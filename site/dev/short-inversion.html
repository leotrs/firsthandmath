1. Setup

Consider two circles, one inside the other, tangent at a point T. Between them lies a chain of circles, C₀, C₁, C₂, and so on, each tangent to the two original circles and the next circle in the chain. Write hₙ for the distance from the center of the n'th circle to the line through T and the centers of the two original circles.

2. The fact

Pappus of Alexandria proved, some 1,700 years ago, that hₙ is exactly n times the diameter of Cₙ, regardless of the relative sizes of the two original circles.

3. Inversion

To see this, we need a construction called inversion. Inversion through a circle of unit radius sends a point P to the point on the ray from the center through P whose distance from the center is one over the distance from P to the center. Inversion turns circles through the center into straight lines, and it keeps tangent circles tangent.

4. The reason

Since D₁ and D₂ pass through T, they become two parallel lines, and the chain becomes a stack of equal circles between them, each touching the next. The image of Cₙ therefore lies n diameters above L. When the circle of inversion crosses Cₙ at right angles, Cₙ is its own image, so Cₙ itself lies n of its own diameters above L.
