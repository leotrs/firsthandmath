About

Firsthand Math is a collection of mathematical explorations. Each page is built around interactive figures, made to be moved, played and changed, so that the reader works with the mathematical objects directly instead of only reading about them.

A piece of mathematics is easier to understand by experiencing it firsthand than by watching someone else handle it. Nodding along is not enough.

What you will find here

There are two kinds of pages. Digestions are long: a recent or important result, worked through step by step, with as many figures as necessary to convey the complex ideas of mathematics. Notes are short: a single idea, with a figure to explore it.

Most topics come from graph theory, linear algebra, computer science, and complexity science. One of the main goals of Firsthand Math is to give as many topics as possible the careful treatment that a few mathematical gems, such as the prime numbers, the Fibonacci sequence and the Pythagorean theorem, have received many times over. Those topics deserve their popularity. Seen from outside the field, though, striking mathematics seems rare.

To the working mathematician, mathematics is always colorful, dynamic and intriguing, and many professional mathematicians report being moved by emotion just as much as by reason. Firsthand Math attempts to convey that feeling to a wider audience.

Inspiration

Explaining mathematics and science with interactive and animated figures is not new, and several earlier projects were direct inspirations for Firsthand Math. Distill (2016 to 2021) was a scientific journal "dedicated to making machine learning clear and dynamic", with interactive figures inside research articles. 3Blue1Brown is Grant Sanderson's YouTube channel of animated mathematics. In his essay Explorable Explanations (2011), Bret Victor made the case for text that readers can manipulate, and Bartosz Ciechanowski and Amit Patel of Red Blob Games have taken that idea further in their interactive articles.

Who writes it

Every page and figure is made with love by Leo Torres, a mathematician, research scientist, software developer, tinkerer and builder, father, amateur boulderer, and terrible home-cook, in Leipzig, Germany.
