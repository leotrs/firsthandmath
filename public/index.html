Turning a circle inside out

Inversion in a circle maps the inside of the circle to the outside and the outside to the inside, sending points near the center far away and far points close to the center. It maps circles through the center to straight lines, and it preserves the angles between curves.

Turán's theorem, from edges to eigenvalues

By Turán's theorem, a graph with no clique of a given size has at most a certain number of edges. In a spectral version of the theorem, the largest eigenvalue of the adjacency matrix is bounded instead, and the original theorem follows from it in two lines.
